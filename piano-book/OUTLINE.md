# Book Outline — DRAFT for review

Principle: every lesson = one new idea + why it works + something to play + a self-check.
History only appears when it explains *why* a rule exists.

## Part 1 — Sound and the Keyboard (no notation yet)
1. What a note is: pitch, vibration, higher/lower
2. The keyboard map: groups of 2 and 3 black keys, finding C
3. The musical alphabet A–G and why it repeats (octaves)
4. Half steps and whole steps — the "unit of measurement" of music
5. Sharps and flats: what the black keys are called
6. Posture and hand shape, explained as a lever system

## Part 2 — Rhythm (time before pitch)
7. Steady beat and the metronome
8. Note lengths: whole, half, quarter
9. Rests: silence is measured too
10. Measures, bar lines, and time signatures (4/4, 3/4, 2/4)
11. Eighth notes and counting "1 & 2 &"
12. Dots and ties: extending notes

## Part 3 — Reading Pitch
13. The staff and the treble clef
14. The bass clef
15. The grand staff and middle C
16. Ledger lines
17. Reading by intervals (steps and skips) instead of letter-by-letter

## Part 4 — Scales and Keys (the "why" behind every song)
18. The major scale formula: W-W-H-W-W-W-H
19. C, G, F major and fingering crossings
20. Key signatures and the circle of fifths
21. Minor scales (natural, harmonic, melodic)
22. Relative major/minor

## Part 5 — Intervals and Chords
23. Naming intervals (2nds through octaves)
24. Triads: major, minor, diminished, augmented
25. Chords built on each scale degree (I, ii, iii, IV, V, vi, vii°)
26. Inversions
27. Seventh chords

## Part 6 — Harmony in Practice
28. Common progressions (I–IV–V–I, I–V–vi–IV)
29. Accompaniment patterns (block, broken, Alberti bass)
30. Playing melody + chords from a lead sheet
31. Cadences: how music "ends a sentence"

## Part 7 — Expression and Technique
32. Dynamics (p, f, crescendo) and articulation (legato, staccato)
33. The sustain pedal
34. Hand independence drills
35. Practice method: slow practice, chunking, metronome ladders

## Part 8 — Toward Pianist
36. Reading longer pieces and form (AB, ABA, rondo)
37. Sixteenth notes, syncopation, triplets
38. All 12 major and minor keys
39. Sight-reading routine
40. A graded repertoire path from here onward

## Every lesson contains
- Concept (short, plain language)
- Why it works (engineering-style explanation where useful)
- Exercise to play
- Self-check: clear pass/fail criteria
